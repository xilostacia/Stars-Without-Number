The **Hivers** are one of the best-known extraterrestrial civilizations encountered by humanity and among the least humanoid. Their bodies, reproduction, language, social organization, and even basic assumptions about leadership differ dramatically from human norms. Despite the human name, Hivers are **not a hive mind**. Every Hiver is a distinct individual with its own personality, interests, relationships, and ambitions. The name originated from early human attempts to describe their communal nests and highly cooperative society and has remained in common use ever since.

Hivers are intensely curious, technologically sophisticated, and particularly famous for two things: **robotics** and **Manipulation**.

## Biology
A Hiver possesses a roughly circular central body built around a modified sixfold radial structure. Six major limbs extend from this central body, though one has become highly specialized as the Hiver's primary sensory limb. An adult can raise this sensory limb to roughly human head height, while the rest of the body remains comparatively close to the ground and may span several meters from limb to limb.

The central body contains the brain and most vital organs beneath a tough internal carapace. This makes the apparent "head" somewhat misleading, since the raised sensory structure does not contain the brain. Five of the six limbs are broadly interchangeable and can be used for walking, manipulating objects, supporting the body, or performing several tasks simultaneously. Each ends in a cluster of flexible digits capable of extremely fine manipulation, meaning Hivers have no meaningful equivalent of human left-handedness or right-handedness.

### The Sensory Limb
One limb has evolved into a specialized sensory and manipulatory organ carrying multiple independently mobile eyes, infrared-sensitive organs, auditory receptors, and flexible digits. The eyes can be directed separately, allowing a Hiver to watch several directions at once or pair them together for depth perception. A Hiver therefore does not need to turn its entire body to look behind itself and can maintain an unusually broad awareness of its surroundings.

The sensory limb can also manipulate objects directly, allowing tools or samples to be brought extremely close to the sensory organs for examination. Their mouth is located on the underside of the central body rather than anywhere near this sensory structure. Hivers also possess no natural vocal apparatus.

## Communication
Native Hiver communication combines **gesture, touch, and written or displayed symbols**. Movements of the limbs and manipulators can carry substantial information, while physical contact adds another layer of meaning. Written Hiver languages are highly developed and are commonly used when communicating across distance or between species.

A conversation between Hivers can therefore appear almost silent to humans. For interaction with humanity and other vocal species, Hivers commonly use electronic vocalizers, translation systems, displays, or robotic intermediaries. Many understand spoken human languages perfectly well but simply cannot naturally reproduce them. This has contributed to the stereotype that Hivers are emotionally flat or expressionless, when in reality humans are simply incapable of perceiving much of their ordinary body language without training.

## Reproduction
Hiver reproduction is radically different from human reproduction and the species possesses only one biological sex. When Hivers meet, they may exchange reproductive material through specialized contact between their bodies, a process humans traditionally refer to as **handshaking** despite its having little resemblance to a human greeting.

A Hiver retains genetic material gathered from many encounters, which later combines within a reproductive structure inside the body to produce genetically diverse larvae. These larvae are not born sapient. Instead, they enter an instinctive juvenile stage and survive independently in suitable wilderness environments, with extremely high mortality.

Those that survive eventually undergo further neurological and behavioral development and return to civilization, where they are accepted into a Hiver nest. A returning juvenile is not necessarily raised by any Hiver genetically related to it, so Hiver concepts of parenthood are primarily social rather than genealogical. The immature larval stage is generally not regarded as a person by Hivers, with personhood associated much more closely with the emergence of sapience and the individual's return to civilization. This distinction remains profoundly uncomfortable to many humans.

## Nests
The fundamental unit of Hiver society is the **nest**. A nest serves simultaneously as something resembling a household, extended family, professional association, social network, and community, and is not defined primarily by genetic descent. Young Hivers returning from their juvenile stage are incorporated into nests and educated by the adults around them.

Individuals may maintain strong attachments to their nest throughout life, but nests themselves are flexible institutions. They can split, combine, specialize, relocate, or establish long-term relationships with other nests. Hiver civilization developed from cooperative communities long before the species became technologically sophisticated, and their ancestors are believed to have lived alongside enormous burrowing animals whose tunnel systems provided shelter while early Hivers cultivated fungi and gathered food.

Cooperation was therefore deeply embedded in Hiver life long before anything resembling modern civilization appeared.

## Psychology
Hivers are sometimes described as unemotional, but this is inaccurate. Their emotions do not map cleanly onto human expressions, and they generally lack many of the competitive status behaviors humans instinctively expect from intelligent beings. They are, however, extraordinarily curious.

Novelty, unfamiliar technology, unexplained phenomena, unexpected social situations, and unusual behavior can attract intense Hiver attention. A Hiver may cross interstellar space simply because something somewhere is sufficiently strange to interest it. This curiosity is one reason Hivers frequently appear far outside regions directly associated with the [[Hive Federation]].

### Violence
Hivers generally dislike direct physical violence and prefer solutions that prevent conflict before it occurs, but this should not be mistaken for helplessness or pacifism. When threatened, they are entirely capable of constructing weapons, organizing defenses, deploying robots, or arranging for someone else to perform dangerous work.

Their preference is to solve a conflict by changing the circumstances that produced it. A Hiver confronted with an enemy might attack that enemy's incentives, finances, logistics, alliances, leadership, political support, or public reputation rather than confront them directly. This preference leads naturally into the feature of Hiver culture most famous among humans.

## Manipulation
To Hivers, **Manipulation** is an art, intellectual discipline, social skill, and source of prestige. A successful Manipulation causes individuals or societies to make choices that produce a desired result without simply forcing them to comply.

A small Manipulation might involve a Hiver wanting a human coworker to adopt a safer maintenance procedure. Rather than arguing repeatedly, the Hiver might change the work schedule, introduce the human to someone who already uses the procedure, ensure better equipment is conveniently available, and allow the human to independently conclude that the new method is preferable.

At the other extreme, Hiver history contains Manipulations directed at corporations, governments, cultures, and entire interstellar societies. The ideal Manipulation is subtle, successful, difficult, and preferably beneficial. Hivers do not necessarily consider this dishonest, since from their perspective every intelligent being constantly influences its environment and other people. Hivers simply study that process deliberately and attempt to become extremely good at it.

This explanation does very little to reassure humans.

### Manipulators
Hivers who specialize in the discipline are commonly called **Manipulators**. Successful Manipulations can grant considerable prestige, particularly when they resolve major problems without coercion or bloodshed. Manipulators study psychology, economics, sociology, politics, communication, history, mathematics, and predictive modeling, and the best are capable of constructing extremely complicated chains of events.

This has created a persistent problem in human-Hiver relations. Whenever something unexpectedly benefits the Hivers, humans frequently assume they planned it. Sometimes they did, sometimes they genuinely did not, and occasionally a Hiver finds the suspicion itself useful.

## Government
Hiver political organization is remarkably decentralized. Traditional Hiver society has little interest in powerful individual rulers, with authority instead emerging from cooperation between nests, specialists, temporary committees, and groups established to solve particular problems.

Humans sometimes struggle to determine who is actually "in charge" of a Hiver organization because the answer is often **nobody in the human sense**. Groups form around specific issues, gather whatever expertise and information are necessary, coordinate a response, and may dissolve once the problem has been resolved.

This system allows Hivers to maintain extremely large political structures without placing much cultural value on personal political dominance. That does not make Hiver politics simple. It merely makes them complicated in ways humans are less accustomed to.

## The Hive Federation
The largest Hiver political sphere is generally known to humans as the [[Hive Federation]]. Despite the name, it is not simply a Hiver empire. Numerous species, governments, worlds, commercial networks, and autonomous communities participate within it while retaining substantial control over their own internal institutions and cultures.

Hivers sit near the center of this enormous network without behaving like conventional imperial rulers. This has naturally led outsiders to ask whether the Federation's member societies genuinely chose their relationships with the Hivers or were carefully Manipulated into doing so. Hivers generally regard the distinction as considerably less important than humans do.

## Technology
Hiver civilization is particularly advanced in **automation, computation, communication, and robotics**. Robots are ubiquitous throughout Hiver society and perform industrial work, maintenance, construction, transportation, hazardous operations, scientific research, domestic tasks, and military functions.

A Hiver vessel may require remarkably few biological crew because enormous portions of its normal operation are automated. This does not mean Hivers necessarily regard robots as people or attempt to make them resemble biological beings. Most view automation as the obvious solution to work that does not require a sophont, and their robots are commonly designed entirely around function.

A reactor-inspection machine does not need a face. A cargo robot does not need legs if wheels, tracks, manipulators, or some stranger arrangement accomplish the job more efficiently. Human visitors consequently find Hiver environments crowded with specialized machines whose purpose is not always immediately obvious.

### Computers and Information
Hiver expertise in computation is equally formidable. Their mathematicians, programmers, language theorists, communication specialists, and systems engineers are widely respected throughout known space.

Translation technology is an especially important field because Hiver civilization has long required communication between radically different forms of intelligent life. Hiver-designed translation systems are therefore among the most sophisticated known. Their technology is not universally superior to human engineering, but in the fields Hivers specialize in, human companies frequently compete against centuries of extremely mature development.

## Ships
Hiver spacecraft reflect Hiver biology rather than human expectations. Traditional human layouts built around corridors, forward-facing consoles, chairs, and two-handed controls are inefficient for a radially symmetrical species with five interchangeable locomotive and manipulatory limbs.

Hiver control stations often surround the operator rather than face in one direction, displays may be distributed across several viewing angles simultaneously, and robotic systems perform much of the routine physical work. Their ships also tend to make extensive use of automation, meaning relatively little interior volume needs to be devoted to conventional crew workspaces.

Mixed-species ships exist, but vessels intended primarily for Hivers can feel extremely strange to human passengers. The Hivers themselves generally find human spacecraft unnecessarily labor-intensive.

## Relations with Humanity
Humanity's relationship with the Hivers is generally peaceful, though rarely entirely comfortable. Humans value Hiver robotics, electronics, translation systems, mathematics, and scientific expertise, while Hivers find humanity intensely interesting.

Human political behavior, cultural diversity, risk-taking, warfare, rapidly changing corporations, and tendency to repeatedly solve problems in ways Hivers would never have considered provide almost limitless material for observation. The greatest source of friction remains Manipulation.

Human governments routinely assume Hiver diplomatic missions are influencing local politics, corporate executives worry that apparently harmless Hiver consultants may be shaping entire markets, and intelligence agencies devote substantial resources to detecting Hiver social engineering. The Hivers consider much of this paranoia excessive, while also acknowledging that some of it is entirely justified.

## Relations with the K'kree
Relations between the Hivers and the [[K'kree]] have historically been tense. The two species are almost opposites in temperament. K'kree society is intensely herd-oriented, conservative, physically imposing, and deeply hostile toward predatory species, while Hivers are decentralized, experimental, physically nonconfrontational, and fascinated by changing social systems.

Direct military confrontation between the civilizations has generally been avoided. Part of the reason is the Hivers' reputation for Manipulation. The possibility that prolonged interaction with Hivers could itself alter K'kree society is disturbing enough to many K'kree that separation is preferable to conquest.

The Hivers find this reaction entirely reasonable.

## Individual Hivers
Not every Hiver is a master Manipulator, roboticist, scientist, or diplomat. There are Hiver merchants, engineers, explorers, academics, physicians, technicians, artists, pilots, criminals, eccentrics, and wanderers. Some leave Hiver civilization specifically because they are curious about other peoples.

A Hiver living aboard a predominantly human vessel is unusual but hardly unheard of. Their anatomy requires some equipment adaptation, but they breathe compatible atmospheres and can generally inhabit human environments without the extensive life-support modifications required by species such as the [[Githiaskio]].

The larger obstacle is usually cultural. A human crewmember may eventually discover that the Hiver has spent the past four months carefully arranging circumstances so they finally ask for a promotion, apologize to their sister, stop gambling, or replace the ship's unsafe coolant pump. The Hiver will probably be delighted that everything worked.

The human's reaction is considerably less predictable.

## Playability
Hivers are fully suitable as player characters, though their radically nonhuman anatomy requires some accommodation. Their radial body plan, multiple manipulatory limbs, and lack of natural speech mean that human-designed furniture, controls, clothing, and equipment may need modification or replacement. Most Hiver characters rely on electronic vocalizers or translation systems when dealing with species that communicate primarily through speech.

Their physiology makes them well suited to technical work, robotics, science, engineering, investigation, diplomacy, and other roles that reward multitasking and broad situational awareness. Their multiple limbs allow them to handle several physical tasks efficiently, but this should not be treated as granting a full independent action for every limb.

Hiver characters are not required to be Manipulators, scientists, or roboticists. Those are major parts of Hiver civilization, not mandatory personalities. A player Hiver might be a merchant, explorer, pilot, doctor, criminal, technician, academic, or simply an unusually curious individual who has chosen to spend time among other species.

Their largest challenge is usually cultural rather than environmental. Hivers can survive comfortably in most human habitats, but their silent body language, unusual social instincts, decentralized view of authority, and casual acceptance of Manipulation can create significant misunderstandings with human crews.
