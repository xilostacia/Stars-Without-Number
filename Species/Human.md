**Humans** are by far the most numerous and politically dominant species in known space. By the 32nd and 33rd centuries, humanity has spread across hundreds of inhabited systems and established permanent populations on planets, moons, orbital habitats, stations, asteroids, ships, and deep-space installations. Human civilization is enormous, culturally diverse, and politically fragmented, with no single government claiming authority over the species as a whole. Nations, alliances, independent worlds, colonial governments, corporations, private military organizations, criminal networks, and isolated frontier settlements all occupy the same expanding network of human space.

[[Earth]] remains inhabited and enormously important to human culture, politics, and history, but it is no longer the demographic or industrial center of humanity. Centuries of migration, colonization, resource extraction, and industrial development shifted increasingly large portions of the human population away from the homeworld. Earth was never abandoned or rendered irrelevant. Humanity simply became far too large and geographically dispersed for one planet to remain the center of everything.

## Earth
Earth remains the ancestral home of humanity and one of the most recognizable worlds in known space. Many major governments, military traditions, corporations, languages, religions, and cultural identities still trace themselves directly back to Earth, and events on the planet continue to influence human politics far beyond the Solar System. For billions of humans born elsewhere, however, Earth is a place of historical and cultural importance rather than somewhere they have ever personally visited.

Humanity's departure from Earth happened gradually rather than through a single evacuation or collapse. Population pressure, ecological strain, competition over resources, political ambitions, and the increasing economic practicality of living beyond Earth all encouraged offworld expansion. Early settlements depended heavily upon Earth, but later colonies developed their own agriculture, industries, shipyards, governments, and populations. Eventually entire regions of human civilization existed that could function indefinitely without direct support from the homeworld.

The result is an Earth that remains powerful without being the unquestioned capital of humanity. Some people still regard it as the center of human civilization, while others consider that attitude outdated or even provincial. On distant colonies, Earth may be discussed with the same mixture of respect, resentment, nostalgia, and indifference that once surrounded the old homelands of terrestrial colonial populations.

## Human Expansion
Human expansion began within the Solar System, where orbital habitats, mining operations, lunar settlements, planetary colonies, and industrial stations gradually transformed space from somewhere people visited into somewhere people permanently lived. Once large offworld populations became economically sustainable, continued expansion became self-reinforcing. More settlements created more industry, more industry produced more ships, and those ships allowed increasingly distant settlements to survive without relying upon Earth for every major resource.

The development of practical faster-than-light travel changed the scale of this expansion completely. Early human FTL technology was heavily influenced by recovered [[Roswells|Roswell]] technology, although the systems eventually produced by humanity developed into technologies humans could manufacture and operate independently. Interstellar expansion accelerated rapidly once reliable travel between neighboring systems became commercially and politically practical.

Humanity did not spread evenly in every direction. Settlement followed the routes that ships could actually use, producing dense corridors of civilization separated by regions that remain comparatively isolated. Some systems developed into enormous population centers because they sat at natural intersections in the interstellar network, while other potentially valuable worlds remained sparsely populated because reaching them was inconvenient.

## Relay Routes
Human interstellar travel depends upon established **relay routes**. Ships cannot simply point toward any visible star and perform an unrestricted jump across interstellar space. Travel instead follows a network of usable routes connecting particular systems, and the shape of this network has had an enormous influence on economics, warfare, settlement, and politics.

Systems positioned along several important routes often become major ports regardless of whether their planets are particularly remarkable. Trade, passengers, military traffic, fuel, repairs, warehouses, shipyards, and financial services accumulate around transportation hubs simply because enormous numbers of ships must pass through them. Conversely, a physically nearby system may be effectively remote if the relay network requires several indirect jumps to reach it.

Relay geography creates natural chokepoints. Governments regulate border crossings, corporations charge transit fees, militaries fortify strategically important systems, and pirates prey upon traffic where ships have limited alternative routes. Wars can be decided by controlling systems that would otherwise possess little economic value, while frontier colonies can remain surprisingly isolated despite sitting only a few jumps from heavily populated regions.

## Human Worlds
Most humans live on planets or moons that can support human habitation to some degree. A small number are close enough to Earth conditions that people can comfortably live outdoors without specialized equipment, while many more require environmental assistance ranging from simple atmospheric processing to completely sealed settlements. Terraforming exists, but transforming an entire world remains vastly more difficult than modifying a settlement or constructing infrastructure suited to local conditions.

Other humans live permanently in orbital habitats, asteroid settlements, artificial environments, industrial stations, generation-old ships, military installations, mining colonies, and deep-space facilities. These populations are no longer considered temporary extensions of planetary civilization. Many have existed for centuries and possess cultures, dialects, political traditions, and identities that developed entirely in space.

Human settlements vary enormously in scale. A major core system may contain billions of inhabitants distributed across several worlds and hundreds of habitats, while a frontier system might contain one settlement with only a few thousand permanent residents. Both are considered ordinary parts of human civilization.

## Gravity and Human Variation
Humanity remains one species, but centuries of living under different gravitational conditions have produced visible variation between populations. These differences are generally treated the same way humans have historically treated other environmental and regional physical differences rather than as evidence that humanity has divided into separate species.

People raised under higher gravity tend to be shorter, heavier, and more densely built. Greater muscular and skeletal strength is useful simply for ordinary movement, and long-established high-gravity populations can develop very compact physiques. Low-gravity populations tend in the opposite direction, with taller bodies, lighter builds, and longer limbs becoming increasingly common.

These differences are not absolute. Genetics, childhood environment, exercise, medicine, nutrition, artificial gravity, and individual variation all influence development. A person's appearance may suggest the environment in which they were raised, but it is rarely enough to identify their exact homeworld.

## Spacers
**Spacers** are the most visually distinctive of the major human environmental populations. The term is usually applied to people whose families have spent generations living primarily aboard ships, orbital habitats, asteroid colonies, stations, or other environments where gravity is consistently lower than Earth standard. Spacer populations vary widely, but extreme height and relatively light builds are common enough to have become strongly associated with them.

A spacer around seven feet tall is normal, particularly among populations whose childhood development occurs almost entirely under reduced gravity. They are not malformed or unnaturally stretched. Their proportions remain recognizably human, but long limbs, narrow frames, comparatively low body mass, and exceptional height can make them immediately noticeable beside humans raised on Earth-standard worlds.

Spacers are not biologically incapable of visiting planets, but high gravity can be unpleasant or physically exhausting for someone accustomed to much lighter environments. Exercise programs, medication, support equipment, artificial-gravity conditioning, and gradual acclimation are commonly used by spacers who expect to spend significant periods planetside. The reverse also occurs, with people raised under high gravity finding very low-gravity environments disorienting or physically awkward until they adapt.

The word spacer also has a cultural meaning that does not perfectly correspond to body type. Someone born and raised aboard a major station may strongly identify as a spacer even if that station maintains near-standard gravity, while a very tall person from a low-gravity planetary colony may not consider themselves one at all. Among long-established spacer communities, ships and stations are not temporary environments or workplaces. They are simply home.

## Human Cultures
There is no universal human culture. Thousands of languages, religions, national identities, ethnic traditions, regional cultures, colonial societies, immigrant communities, and political movements exist throughout human space. Some remain closely connected to traditions originating on Earth, while others developed centuries after their founders left the homeworld.

Major Earth populations remained important during interstellar colonization rather than blending into one generic space culture. American, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, British, Australian, Indian, Western European, African, Latin American, Southeast Asian, Southwest Asian, Arabic-speaking, and countless other communities established settlements throughout the period of expansion. Some colonies were internationally mixed from their founding, while others began with populations drawn heavily from one country, region, language, or cultural community.

Centuries of separation changed these cultures without necessarily erasing their origins. A colony founded primarily by Japanese settlers may still speak Japanese and retain recognizable cultural traditions while possessing political institutions, food, architecture, slang, clothing, and social customs that never existed in Japan itself. Another population may preserve only fragments of its founders' original identity after centuries of mixing with neighboring worlds.

Human culture therefore becomes more diverse rather than less diverse as distance from Earth increases.

## Politics
There is no unified human government. Human space consists of overlapping states, alliances, independent systems, colonies, corporate territories, protectorates, military jurisdictions, and settlements that may possess little meaningful government beyond their own local institutions. Large powers can influence dozens of systems, while some inhabited worlds remain politically independent despite populations smaller than a single city on Earth.

The [[United Colonies Security Authority]] is one of the largest military organizations in human space, but it does not govern humanity. It functions more like an enormous multinational security and military alliance whose members retain their own governments and political interests. Other major human powers maintain their own militaries, alliances, colonial relationships, trade agreements, and strategic territories.

Jurisdiction becomes increasingly complicated in systems containing multiple governments and private interests. A single star system may contain an independent planetary government, foreign military facilities, corporate stations, privately owned habitats, and settlements whose legal status remains disputed. Which laws apply can depend on where someone is standing, who owns the installation, what ship they arrived aboard, and which agreements the relevant governments currently recognize.

## Corporations
Corporations are enormously powerful throughout human space, but they have not replaced governments. Major companies can own entire industrial settlements, orbital stations, shipyards, mines, agricultural operations, research installations, transportation networks, prisons, security forces, and even colonies. The largest corporations possess more wealth and infrastructure than some independent states.

Corporate authority varies enormously depending upon location. On an established core world, even a huge corporation may operate under strict local regulation. On a remote company settlement, the corporation may own the housing, power system, medical facilities, starport, communications network, security service, and nearly every major employer in the colony. The distinction between economic power and political power becomes increasingly thin in such places.

## The Core
The oldest and most important human systems are heavily developed. Major worlds support enormous populations alongside dense orbital infrastructure, transportation networks, military facilities, universities, financial centers, hospitals, industrial complexes, and shipyards. Some systems have been continuously inhabited for centuries and contain infrastructure built over dozens of generations.

Interstellar travel is ordinary in these regions. Starports may process thousands of passengers and enormous quantities of cargo every day, and ships arriving from another system attract little attention. Aliens are still uncommon compared with humans, but major ports are among the few places where seeing one may not be remarkable.

Core life is not necessarily glamorous. Crowded cities, corporate employment, organized crime, political corruption, poverty, bureaucracy, rent, pollution, and ordinary human problems survived the invention of FTL. Humanity became interstellar without ceasing to be humanity.

## The Frontier
The frontier is considerably less controlled. Newly settled worlds may contain only mining camps, research bases, agricultural settlements, company towns, or a single starport surrounded by thousands of kilometers of wilderness. Governments that appear powerful on maps may possess very little practical authority in such places because meaningful assistance can be several jumps away.

Frontier society consequently has a strong **wild-west character**. Independent crews transport cargo and passengers between isolated settlements. Prospectors search for valuable resources. Corporations compete for claims. Smugglers exploit borders and regulations. Pirates prey upon shipping. Local law enforcement may consist of a few officers trying to maintain order across an area larger than some terrestrial countries.

Frontier worlds can be dangerous, poorly supplied, and politically unstable, but they also offer opportunities difficult to find in established systems. A person with enough money, skill, luck, or willingness to take risks can acquire land, establish a business, discover valuable resources, or become locally important far more easily than they could in the core.

## Ships and Independent Crews
Starships are the connective tissue of human civilization. Cargo vessels, passenger ships, couriers, survey craft, military vessels, mining ships, prison transports, research vessels, private yachts, smugglers, pirates, and countless specialized designs move constantly through the relay network.

Independent crews are especially common beyond the core systems. A privately operated vessel may carry cargo on one journey, passengers on another, perform a survey contract afterward, transport equipment for a corporation, and later accept work whose legality depends heavily on which jurisdiction is being asked. Small crews frequently survive by taking whatever work keeps the ship fueled, maintained, and moving.

For many people, owning or controlling a starship represents one of the few realistic ways to remain genuinely independent. A functioning vessel allows its crew to leave employers, governments, debts, enemies, or simply places they no longer wish to remain. The freedom is expensive, dangerous, and constantly threatened by maintenance costs, but it remains freedom.

## Technology
Human technology is extremely advanced but usually remains practical and recognizable. Computers, firearms, vehicles, robotics, medical technology, genetic engineering, artificial intelligence, spacecraft, industrial equipment, communications systems, and advanced materials have improved enormously, but most operate according to principles an educated human engineer can understand.

Human technology is particularly good at standardization and mass production. Equipment is designed to be manufactured in enormous quantities, repaired with available parts, transported through established logistics networks, and used by people who may have received only basic training. This industrial scale is one of humanity's greatest advantages.

Alien technology can be considerably stranger. Some alien devices use unusual materials, unfamiliar interfaces, biological components, or engineering principles that human industry cannot reproduce reliably. Human scientists may understand what a device accomplishes long before they understand how to manufacture another one.

## Weapons
Projectile weapons remain common throughout human space because they are cheap, effective, reliable, and easy to manufacture. Centuries of development have improved ammunition, recoil systems, targeting equipment, materials, propellants, and weapon electronics without eliminating the usefulness of throwing a physical projectile at high velocity.

Lasers and other advanced weapons exist, particularly among military forces and specialized organizations, but they have not completely replaced conventional firearms. A mercenary on a frontier world may carry a weapon whose materials, optics, ammunition, and internal systems would amaze a modern gunsmith while still recognizing it fundamentally as a rifle or shotgun.

This practical approach appears throughout human military technology. Humanity frequently prefers something reliable, repairable, and available in huge numbers over something theoretically superior but difficult to maintain.

## Aliens
Alien life is an accepted part of human reality. Most educated humans can identify several major alien species and know at least basic information about them from school, media, government publications, entertainment, or simple cultural exposure.

Personally meeting an alien remains much less common. Humanity's enormous population means that large regions of human space are overwhelmingly human, and someone can live their entire life knowing that [[Kynari]], [[Raukha]], [[Aezorgh]], [[Roswells]], [[Hivers]], and other aliens exist without ever actually encountering one.

Major ports, diplomatic centers, research institutions, and important trade systems are more cosmopolitan, but humanity remains numerically dominant. First contact with a previously unknown intelligent alien species is therefore still possible and remains one of the most significant events an explorer, scientist, government, or starship crew could encounter.

## Ancient Alien Contact
Humanity encountered aliens before it possessed any concept of extraterrestrial civilization. The [[Raukha]] invaded ancient Earth and successfully conquered Egypt before their occupation eventually collapsed and the surviving invasion force was destroyed. The event disappeared into mythology, with several Raukha individuals becoming remembered as divine figures rather than extraterrestrial conquerors.

[[Kynari]] contact occurred differently. Small groups, explorers, stranded crews, mercenaries, and temporary settlements appeared sporadically across human history rather than through any coordinated invasion. Some of these encounters contributed to stories of Cynocephali and other canine humanoids.

Most ancient myths have nothing to do with aliens. The discovery that a few stories contained distorted memories of genuine extraterrestrial encounters did not suddenly turn every god, monster, legendary kingdom, or supernatural creature into evidence of alien contact.

## Uplift
Humanity eventually moved from discovering intelligent alien life to deliberately creating it. [[Neogen]] achieved the first major successful human uplift roughly two centuries ago through the creation of the [[Githiaskio]], an intelligent species descended from terrestrial octopuses.

The program followed numerous less successful experiments, including attempts involving primates. The creation of the Githiaskio permanently changed arguments surrounding genetic engineering, personhood, artificial species, corporate responsibility, and whether humanity possessed any moral right to intentionally create new intelligent life.

Whatever the philosophical arguments, the result cannot be undone. The Githiaskio are now an independent people with their own lives, communities, and place within known space.

## Human Travellers
Humans are the most common Travellers simply because there are vastly more humans than any other known population. Merchants, pilots, soldiers, diplomats, criminals, scientists, doctors, engineers, mercenaries, explorers, miners, colonists, refugees, prospectors, corporate employees, and people with no particular plan beyond leaving their current world constantly move through the relay network.

Human Traveller crews can contain people whose backgrounds would once have placed them on opposite sides of Earth. A seven-foot station-born spacer mechanic, a heavily built mercenary raised under stronger gravity, and a retired corporate lawyer from a wealthy core world might own the same ship and consider nothing unusual about the arrangement. Their homeworlds, cultures, careers, and physical builds can differ enormously while all three remain ordinary humans.
