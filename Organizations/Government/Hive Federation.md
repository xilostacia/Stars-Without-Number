The **Hive Federation** is the principal interstellar civilization of the [[Hivers]] and one of the oldest surviving political entities known to humanity. Despite its name, it bears little resemblance to the centralized federations familiar to most humans. There is no president, monarch, parliament, supreme council, or permanent central authority governing Federation space. Instead, the Hive Federation consists of thousands of worlds, communities, organizations, and alien societies connected through overlapping networks of cooperation.

The term _Hive Federation_ itself originated outside Hiver society. Hivers generally have little need for a single name describing the entirety of their civilization, and _Hive Confederation_ is also sometimes encountered in older human writing. Both terms describe the same broad political and cultural sphere rather than a conventional state with clearly defined layers of government.

Hiver society is based around organizations traditionally translated as **nests**. A nest is a voluntary association of individuals gathered around a particular activity, occupation, project, business, scientific field, social purpose, or shared interest. Some nests consist of only a handful of individuals. Others operate across multiple worlds and contain enormous numbers of members. A Hiver may leave one nest, join another, or associate with several organizations over the course of its life.

Much of what humans would consider government therefore develops from interaction between nests. Industrial organizations establish manufacturing standards, transportation groups coordinate shipping practices, scientific organizations exchange research, and communities establish whatever institutions are necessary to maintain local order. When enough organizations agree that a particular practice is useful, it may spread throughout Federation space without any legislature ever formally declaring it law.

This produces a political system that outsiders sometimes find nearly impossible to understand. Authority certainly exists within the Federation, but it is usually attached to a particular responsibility rather than to a permanent office. An organization responsible for maintaining a spaceport may possess nearly absolute authority concerning that spaceport while having no authority whatsoever over education, commerce, or local law. Another organization may handle those matters instead. Jurisdiction frequently overlaps, and disputes are usually resolved through negotiation between the organizations involved.

The result is remarkably stable. The Federation does not depend upon the survival of a dynasty, political party, constitution, or ruling class. Governments may change on individual member worlds without fundamentally affecting the greater Federation. Organizations disappear and new ones replace them. Policies spread, change, or gradually fall out of use. The Federation persists because the network connecting its members persists.

This decentralization does not mean that Federation society lacks major institutions. Three organizations in particular perform functions broad enough that outsiders generally treat them as branches of a federal government: the **Embassies Directorate**, the **Federation Development Agency**, and the **Federation Navy**. Hivers themselves are less likely to make such a distinction. These organizations are simply unusually large nests tasked with responsibilities that affect nearly every part of Federation society. Hivers may enter or leave federal service much as they would join or leave other major organizations.

The **Embassies Directorate** maintains one of the most extensive diplomatic and cultural networks in known space. A Hiver embassy is considerably more than a diplomatic mission assigned to a foreign government. Embassies travel constantly between Federation worlds, carrying personnel, information, biological material, cultural developments, scientific discoveries, commercial knowledge, and news from one population to another. Their movement helps prevent the many Hiver populations scattered across interstellar distances from becoming isolated from one another.

Historically, the embassy system also served an important biological purpose. Hiver populations separated across many worlds required continued exchange to preserve the broad genetic continuity of the species. The organized movement of Hivers between worlds became an established institution and eventually grew into the enormous diplomatic and cultural network known today. Other Federation species participate in the embassy system as well, although their traditions and biological requirements naturally differ from those of the Hivers.

Embassies have consequently become one of the primary mechanisms through which the Federation maintains cohesion. An embassy may arrive carrying scientists, artists, merchants, diplomats, specialists, travelers, researchers, or individuals seeking association with organizations on another world. Some remain only briefly, while others become temporary communities in their own right. The constant movement of these groups spreads information and practices throughout Federation space without requiring a centralized bureaucracy to direct them.

Hiver embassies also frequently serve as mediators. Political disputes, commercial conflicts, scientific disagreements, and disagreements between member societies can attract the involvement of neutral organizations whose members have no direct stake in the original dispute. Hiver legal tradition generally favors negotiated solutions whenever possible, and respected specialists may be invited to interpret precedent or propose settlements.

Law within the Federation varies considerably from world to world. Member societies retain their own governments and legal systems, while Hiver communities often rely upon combinations of customary practice, contractual relationships, organizational rules, and arbitration. Federation-wide restrictions tend to concern matters that genuinely require interstellar coordination, particularly navigation, defense, dangerous technologies, first contact, and relations between member civilizations.

The Federation does possess mechanisms for taxation, although even these operate differently from many human systems. Major federal organizations receive resources from the societies and organizations benefiting from their services. Payment does not always take the form of currency. An industrial nest might provide equipment, a shipbuilding organization might construct vessels for the Federation Navy, and a research organization might provide expertise or information. Hivers tend to regard these exchanges less as compulsory taxation than as the contribution required to maintain institutions from which the Federation collectively benefits.

The broader Hiver economy can be equally confusing to outsiders. Hiver economic relationships are heavily based around nests and systems of credit associated with them. Individuals work, obtain resources, and move between organizations within a system that has sometimes been described by human economists as combining characteristics usually associated with both communal and market economies. Considerable private enterprise exists, Hivers value material comfort, and commercial organizations can become extremely wealthy, but wealth and obligation are often recorded through relationships between individuals and nests rather than solely through personal accumulation.

Federation industry is particularly respected for its communications systems, computers, translation technology, mathematics, automation, and advanced electronics. Hiver mathematical and linguistic systems have spread far beyond Federation territory, and Hiver-designed translators are encountered throughout regions that regularly trade with them. Their technological reputation has made Federation universities, research organizations, and technical institutions attractive even to outsiders who have little understanding of Hiver society.

The second major interstellar institution is the **Federation Development Agency**. The Agency coordinates exploration, planetary surveys, first contact, development assistance, and Federation involvement with civilizations considered technologically or socially less developed. Its activities extend from straightforward scientific expeditions to projects lasting generations.

Hiver attitudes toward developing civilizations are strongly influenced by their powerful parental instincts. When early Hiver explorers discovered other intelligent species, many responded not with conquest but with a desire to assist them. Over time this became an organized policy of technological, educational, economic, and social development. The Federation has helped numerous smaller civilizations enter interstellar society, many of which later became members or close associates of the Federation.

This attitude is not universally appreciated. Critics, particularly outside the Federation, have accused Hivers of paternalism and of assuming that they are qualified to determine how other civilizations should develop. Hivers generally respond that allowing an avoidable catastrophe to occur simply because intervention might be considered impolite is hardly a defensible position.

The most controversial expression of this policy is the Federation's system of **quarantine worlds**. Certain civilizations encountered by Hiver explorers have been judged too dangerous, unstable, or aggressive for unrestricted contact with the wider interstellar community. Such worlds may be isolated from interstellar traffic and prevented from discovering the full extent of civilization beyond their own systems.

Quarantine does not necessarily mean noninterference. It is publicly known that Federation organizations conduct covert programs intended to influence quarantined societies toward conditions considered suitable for eventual contact. Several formerly quarantined civilizations have eventually been admitted into wider Federation society after substantial cultural changes. The policy remains controversial both inside and outside the Federation, particularly because no universal agreement exists concerning when intervention becomes unacceptable interference.

These programs are closely associated with the most famous and frequently misunderstood aspect of Hiver society: **Manipulation**.

Manipulation is the deliberate alteration of circumstances in order to encourage a particular outcome. It may involve psychology, sociology, economics, diplomacy, communication, education, logistics, or almost any other field. Rather than directly ordering someone to perform an action, a skilled Manipulator attempts to create circumstances under which that person independently chooses the desired course.

Manipulation exists at every scale of Hiver life. A Hiver might manipulate a colleague into accepting a new position, encourage two organizations to cooperate, redirect a commercial dispute, or attempt to alter an entire society. Some Manipulations take minutes. Others may continue for decades.

To many humans, the practice sounds inherently deceptive. Hivers do not generally share that assumption. Manipulation is openly recognized as a legitimate intellectual and social discipline within their culture. The ethical question is usually not whether someone influenced another person's decisions, since persuasion of some kind occurs in every society, but what methods were used, what consequences resulted, and whether the Manipulator correctly understood what it was doing.

Hiver Manipulators have acquired an almost mythical reputation outside Federation space. Every unexpected political development involving a Hiver eventually produces someone claiming that the entire event was planned decades in advance. Most Hivers regard these theories with amusement. Hivers are capable of extraordinary long-term social planning, but they are neither omniscient nor capable of controlling every individual around them.

The uncertainty nevertheless remains part of dealing with them. A Hiver may openly admit that it is attempting to influence someone while refusing to explain exactly how. Two completely unrelated events may genuinely be unrelated, or they may be pieces of a project whose purpose will not become apparent for years. Foreign diplomats quickly learn that attempting to discover a hidden Hiver objective behind every conversation is an excellent way to become exhausted.

The **Federation Navy** is the third major institution shared across Federation space and the Federation's principal military force. The Navy exists primarily for collective defense, protection of major interstellar routes, and threats beyond the ability of individual member worlds to address. It is not traditionally used as an instrument of territorial conquest.

Hivers developed organized interstellar warfare relatively late in their history. Violence between Hiver societies was historically uncommon, and Hivers possess a strong aversion to direct personal danger. Contact with aggressive interstellar civilizations eventually demonstrated that peaceful intentions alone were insufficient protection, leading to the creation of permanent defensive forces.

Hiver military doctrine consequently emphasizes exactly the forms of warfare they find most tolerable: sensors, information, automation, drones, robotics, long-range weapons, electronic warfare, superior positioning, overwhelming preparation, and destroying an enemy before direct combat becomes necessary. Federation commanders generally view a fair battle as evidence that something has gone wrong.

The Navy contains personnel from many Federation species. Hivers are perfectly capable of military service, particularly in technical, command, intelligence, logistical, and naval roles, but Federation ground forces have historically depended heavily upon other member species better suited temperamentally and physically to close combat. The Federation makes little ideological distinction between a Hiver-built warship commanded by a mixed crew and infantry drawn from another member species. Both are simply parts of the same defensive system.

The Federation's best-known historical conflict was its war with the [[K'kree]]. The aggressively expansionist K'kree encountered the Federation during their own expansion and attacked several Federation worlds. The Hivers constructed fleets and fought conventionally when necessary, but the conflict became famous for its conclusion. Rather than attempting to defeat the K'kree solely through military force, the Federation demonstrated its ability and willingness to destabilize fundamental elements of K'kree society through Manipulation. The threat proved sufficiently serious that the conflict ended without either civilization needing to wage a prolonged war of extermination.

The war became an enduring example of Hiver strategic thinking. A Hiver military solution does not necessarily involve destroying an opponent's fleet. If changing the reason that fleet was launched is cheaper, safer, and more permanent, many Hivers consider that the superior weapon.

Despite being created by Hivers, the Federation is not an exclusively Hiver civilization. Numerous alien peoples belong to it, some of whom joined after receiving Hiver development assistance while others entered through diplomacy, migration, trade, or political association. Member species are formally regarded as equal participants rather than subjects of a Hiver empire.

In practice, the Federation inevitably reflects Hiver assumptions because the system itself originated with them. Species comfortable with decentralized authority, negotiated relationships, and overlapping organizations generally integrate more easily than species accustomed to strict hierarchies. Member civilizations nevertheless retain substantial freedom to maintain their own governments, cultures, religions, economies, and social structures.

A Federation world might therefore be governed by institutions that look nothing like those of a neighboring system. One may possess a centralized planetary government, another may consist of autonomous cities, and another may have no institution recognizable to humans as a conventional state at all. All can participate in the Federation so long as their governments can interact with the wider network and respect the agreements required for interstellar cooperation.

This arrangement makes determining the Federation's exact borders surprisingly difficult. Some systems are unquestionably part of it. Others are autonomous civilizations tied to Federation organizations through hundreds of treaties and commercial relationships. Still others host Hiver settlements or Federation facilities without being Federation members themselves. Human maps generally draw a convenient political boundary around all of these relationships, even when the reality is considerably less precise.

There is likewise no true **capital** of the Hive Federation. Certain worlds contain particularly important administrative, scientific, commercial, or diplomatic institutions, and outsiders sometimes describe these as capitals for convenience. None serves as the permanent political center from which Federation civilization is ruled.

Foreign relations are conducted through the same networked approach. The Federation maintains conventional embassies when dealing with civilizations that expect them, negotiates treaties, recognizes foreign governments, and participates in interstellar diplomacy. Foreign governments sometimes find this frustrating because identifying who has the authority to negotiate a particular matter may require dealing with several different Hiver organizations.

Once an agreement is reached, however, Federation institutions have a reputation for extraordinary institutional memory. The absence of political parties or election cycles means that a treaty does not suddenly become unpopular because a new administration takes power. Relationships may evolve, but major changes tend to occur gradually.

For an ordinary inhabitant of Federation space, most of this enormous structure is largely invisible. Daily life is usually dominated by a local government, community, nest, business, institution, or species-specific authority. The Federation becomes noticeable when someone travels between systems, works with major interstellar organizations, joins federal service, conducts foreign trade, or becomes involved in an issue spanning several worlds.

This is perhaps the greatest difference between the Hive Federation and the large human powers. Human governments generally begin with political authority and use that authority to organize society. The Hive Federation begins with society organizing itself, and government appears wherever coordination becomes necessary.
