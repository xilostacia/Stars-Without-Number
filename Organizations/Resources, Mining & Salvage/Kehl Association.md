**Kehl Association** is an interstellar salvage and materials-recovery company specializing in derelict spacecraft, abandoned stations, failed colonies, and industrial sites that have outlived their original purpose. Kehl acquires or contracts for these assets, determines what remains economically useful, and returns as much of that material as possible to circulation.

Its crews operate in environments that conventional scrapyards rarely touch. Kehl salvage teams board unstable wrecks, dismantle damaged structures, isolate hazardous systems, recover intact machinery, and strip valuable metals and components before a site deteriorates further. Large wrecks are often processed where they are rather than hauled intact to established industrial centers.

Recovered equipment follows several paths. Functional machinery may be inspected and resold, damaged components can be broken down for reusable parts, and bulk material is sorted and refined before returning to industrial markets. Rare alloys, specialized electronics, reactor components, and other difficult-to-replace equipment can make even badly damaged wrecks commercially valuable.

Kehl maintains mobile processing yards and recovery vessels capable of supporting long-duration work far from established infrastructure. This allows the Association to operate around abandoned colonies, forgotten stations, old battlefields, and remote shipping routes where transporting raw salvage elsewhere would be prohibitively expensive.

The company has developed a reputation for taking on jobs that are dangerous, isolated, or simply too inconvenient for larger industrial firms. Its presence is particularly common in older regions of settled space where centuries of expansion have left behind enormous quantities of obsolete infrastructure.

Kehl's recovered materials frequently re-enter the same industrial supply chains served by manufacturers and resource companies such as [[Hassan Industries]], [[Rimward Extraction]], and [[Vashaw]]. The Association's business depends less on producing anything new than on ensuring that useful material does not remain buried inside something dead.
