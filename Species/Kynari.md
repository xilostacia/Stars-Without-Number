The **Kynari** are a widespread starfaring alien species known for strong social instincts, highly personal loyalties, rapidly shifting hierarchies, and cultures in which reputation often matters as much as formal authority. Their civilizations are old, widespread, and politically fragmented, producing everything from stable planetary governments and commercial powers to mercenary fleets, wandering communities, criminal organizations, and independent starship crews.

Humans almost universally describe the Kynari as canid-like. The comparison is obvious enough, particularly in the shape of the face, mobile ears, teeth, fur, tail, and some aspects of their body language, but it should not be taken literally. Kynari are not wolves or dogs given humanoid bodies, nor are they related to any animal native to [[Earth]]. They evolved independently on another world, and their resemblance to terrestrial canids is simply the closest familiar comparison available to humans.
## Biology
Kynari are upright, warm-blooded aliens with two arms, two legs, a tail, and a body plan that is broadly recognizable to humans without actually being humanlike in its details. Their legs are digitigrade, with the heel raised above the ground, and their hips, spine, shoulders, and chest are all proportioned around that stance. The chest is generally deep, the waist narrower and more flexible, and the shoulders sit somewhat farther forward than those of a human.

Their hands are fully suited to tool use and technological work, although the fingers end in short claws rather than human-style nails. Most Kynari possess a long, muscular tail that contributes to balance and also forms an important part of their body language. A Kynari standing upright still has a distinctly alien silhouette, particularly when seen moving, running, crouching, or turning quickly.

The Kynari head produces the strongest comparison to terrestrial canids, but there is considerable variation between individuals and populations. Most possess a projecting jaw and nose, although muzzle length can range from long and narrow to extremely short, with some Kynari having little more than a slight projection beyond the rest of the face. Skull width, jaw depth, brow structure, cheek shape, and the angle at which the head joins the neck can differ enough that two Kynari may look noticeably different while still being immediately recognizable as members of the same species.

Their ears are mobile and similarly variable. Some are tall and narrow, others broad or rounded, while others sweep backward, partially fold, or sit relatively low against the skull. Kynari eyes are positioned more forward than those of many terrestrial canids, reflecting the needs of an intelligent tool-using species rather than those of an Earth predator. Their jaws retain prominent cutting teeth and large canines inherited from an omnivorous predatory ancestry.

Humans frequently compare particular Kynari to wolves, foxes, jackals, coyotes, or various domestic dogs. These comparisons are only visual shorthand. There are no wolf Kynari, fox Kynari, or dog Kynari. The physical variation belongs to the Kynari themselves and developed naturally across their populations.

## Coat and Appearance
Most Kynari are covered in relatively short fur that lies close to the body. Longer growth is usually concentrated around the cheeks, jaw, neck, shoulders, forearms, upper spine, or tail rather than covering the entire body in a heavy coat. Some individuals have almost uniformly short fur, while others develop pronounced cheek tufts, heavier throat growth, ridges along the neck or back, or thicker fur around the shoulders.

Heavy mane-like coats exist, but they are unusual rather than the standard appearance of the species. This distinction is particularly important to humans, who often imagine Kynari as shaggy wolf-people when most individuals are considerably sleeker.

Kynari coloration is extremely diverse. Grey, black, brown, red, cream, white, muted blue, and combinations of these colors all occur naturally. Patterns include masks, patches, stripes, mottling, dorsal markings, lighter undersides, irregular facial markings, and combinations that have no close terrestrial equivalent. Muzzle length, ear shape, skull proportions, height, body mass, limb length, tail length, coat density, and facial fur also vary between populations.

## Senses
Kynari possess excellent hearing and a highly developed sense of smell. Scent remains an important part of how they understand both environments and other people. A Kynari may notice that someone recently occupied a room, recognize a familiar individual before seeing them, detect unfamiliar substances, or distinguish environmental details that a human would completely overlook.

Their hearing is similarly sensitive, particularly to quiet and distant sounds. Kynari vision is broadly comparable to human vision, but they naturally combine sight, hearing, and smell when interpreting their surroundings. Humans therefore sometimes fail to understand how much information a Kynari is receiving from an apparently ordinary environment.

These senses influence Kynari architecture and technology. Ventilation and air circulation receive more attention than in most human-built environments, since scent can linger for significant periods. Acoustic privacy is also important, and Kynari-designed residential areas, meeting spaces, and starships often include better sound isolation than equivalent human structures.

## Diet
Kynari are omnivores descended from opportunistic predators and scavengers. Meat remains a normal part of their diet, but they are capable of eating a broad range of plant foods and have no biological requirement to consume meat at every meal. Modern Kynari cuisine varies enormously between worlds, cultures, and available ecosystems.

Their dentition can make eating appear aggressive to species unfamiliar with them, particularly when tougher foods are involved, but Kynari generally attach no special significance to this. Their eating habits are no more culturally uniform than those of humanity.

## Social Instinct
Kynari evolved as highly social animals and are naturally comfortable living and working in relatively small groups whose members know one another personally. These groups are commonly translated into human languages as **packs**, although a Kynari pack does not necessarily consist of biological relatives and should not be understood simply as an animal pack transferred into an intelligent society.

A pack may form around a family, starship crew, military unit, group of friends, business, political movement, mercenary company, criminal organization, or any other close association. Some packs last only as long as a particular job or voyage, while others remain together for decades. Kynari can also belong to several overlapping social groups at once, although one will often become particularly important.

Most Kynari dislike prolonged social isolation, but they are fully capable of living and traveling alone. The discomfort is closer to loneliness and social deprivation than an absolute biological inability to function without others.

## Charisma and Reputation
Personal influence plays an unusually important role in Kynari society. Confidence, reputation, competence, success, loyalty, courage, generosity, and the ability to attract followers all contribute to the amount of social weight an individual possesses. Traveller represents this through **Charisma**, which replaces Social Standing for Kynari characters.

Charisma is not physical attractiveness and does not simply mean that someone is friendly or persuasive. A Kynari with high Charisma is someone other people are inclined to listen to, follow, trust, fear, respect, or take seriously. That influence may come from proven competence, a history of success, personal magnetism, important connections, military victories, wealth, generosity, or any combination of these.

Reputation is therefore extremely important. An unknown Kynari who accomplishes something extraordinary may acquire supporters very quickly, while someone associated with repeated failure, cowardice, betrayal, or humiliation may see their influence collapse just as quickly. Reputation is not fixed and can be built, lost, rebuilt, or completely reinvented over the course of a life.

## Leadership
Kynari leadership depends heavily upon continued personal support. Formal titles, ranks, and institutions exist, but they rarely function completely independently of the people occupying them. A leader remains powerful because enough people continue to believe that following them is useful, honorable, profitable, safe, or otherwise worthwhile.

This does not mean that Kynari constantly overthrow one another or spend every conversation competing for dominance. A successful leader may command the same group for decades because competence, loyalty, shared experience, and repeated victories reinforce their position. Leadership becomes unstable when the people beneath it stop believing that the individual in charge deserves to remain there.

Authority is also frequently situational. A starship captain may dominate decisions involving navigation and diplomacy while an engineer effectively takes control during a reactor failure and a military specialist commands during a boarding action. Kynari usually have little difficulty allowing different people to take the lead when circumstances clearly favor different expertise.

Humans sometimes interpret this fluidity as constant social competition. In most Kynari groups, status is established through ordinary interaction rather than endless confrontation.

## Loyalty
Kynari loyalty tends to be intensely personal. An individual may care relatively little about the government, corporation, military, or organization they officially serve while remaining completely devoted to particular people within it. This means that Kynari groups can change formal allegiance without changing much internally.

A military unit might abandon one government and enter the service of another while the same soldiers continue following the same commander. A starship crew may work for several governments, corporations, or criminal organizations during its existence without ever breaking apart. To the Kynari, the enduring relationship may be between the people rather than between those people and whatever institution currently employs them.

Humans sometimes interpret this as unreliability. Kynari often find the human tendency to remain loyal to an abstract institution while openly despising the people controlling it equally strange.

## Government
There is no single Kynari political system. Their worlds have produced republics, monarchies, military governments, corporations, dictatorships, trade leagues, federations, democracies, criminal states, communes, and countless arrangements that do not translate neatly into human terminology.

Political structures often change faster than those of humanity because individuals can accumulate or lose support rapidly. A successful leader may gather followers, ships, money, and territory in a surprisingly short period of time, turning a minor organization into a major regional power. When that leader dies, fails publicly, or loses the loyalty of key supporters, the organization may fragment into several competing successors.

This does not mean Kynari civilization is constantly collapsing. Political change is simply normal. Organizations form, merge, divide, disappear, and are replaced while trade, settlement, technological development, and everyday life continue around them. Maps of Kynari political territory frequently become outdated long before maps of the worlds themselves do.

## Conflict
Kynari conflicts frequently become personal even when they begin over larger political or economic issues. A dispute over territory, trade, law, or resources may become inseparable from the relationship between the individuals leading each side. Removing or replacing one influential person can therefore transform a conflict that previously appeared impossible to resolve.

Challenges, defections, negotiated changes of allegiance, public confrontations, political humiliation, and personal settlements all have established places within Kynari societies. Violence is familiar but not automatically preferred. Convincing enough followers that someone else should be in charge can sometimes accomplish more than killing the current leader.

The same social structure can also make conflicts unpredictable. The death of a leader may end a war immediately, or it may create several successor factions that each claim to inherit the dead leader's followers and territory.

## Corsairs
Independent starships are extremely common throughout Kynari space. Many belong to merchants, explorers, prospectors, couriers, mercenaries, survey crews, or other legitimate operations. Others function outside conventional law as smugglers, raiders, or pirates, and these groups are commonly known as **corsairs**.

A successful corsair captain can build an organization remarkably quickly. Successful raids produce wealth, wealth attracts better ships and equipment, and visible success attracts followers. A minor raiding crew can therefore become a significant regional threat in a relatively short period of time.

The reverse is equally true. A disastrous defeat can shatter the reputation holding such an organization together and cause followers to disappear almost overnight. Not every independent Kynari crew is a corsair, and assuming otherwise is a reliable way to insult legitimate travelers.

## Technology
Kynari technology is fully interstellar and broadly compatible with equipment used by humanity and other major alien species. Their engineering is not based upon fundamentally different physical principles, but their equipment is naturally designed around Kynari anatomy.

Seats accommodate tails and digitigrade legs. Pressure suits and armor leave appropriate space for the jaw and ears. Weapon grips, tools, and control surfaces account for clawed hands, while environmental systems place greater emphasis on ventilation and acoustic control.

Kynari ships are especially prone to extensive modification. Independent crews commonly replace systems with whatever equipment they can obtain rather than maintaining a vessel according to its original specifications. A sufficiently old Kynari ship may contain components made by several manufacturers and several different species, all repaired and rebuilt repeatedly over decades of service.

## Early Contact with Earth
Kynari reached [[Earth]] thousands of years before humanity became a starfaring civilization. Unlike the Raukha occupation of ancient Egypt, there was never a major organized Kynari invasion or attempt to rule humanity.

Contact occurred sporadically across long periods of time. Explorers visited Earth, damaged vessels became stranded, small expeditions remained for months or years, and individual mercenaries or travelers occasionally became involved in local events. Some Kynari groups may have established temporary settlements before eventually leaving, dying out, or disappearing into the historical record.

These encounters occurred in different regions and different periods and were never part of a unified Kynari presence. Most humans who saw them had no concept of extraterrestrial life and interpreted the visitors through whatever cultural framework was available.

## Cynocephali
Ancient human traditions concerning the **Cynocephali**, legendary dog-headed peoples supposedly inhabiting distant regions of Africa and Asia, originated at least partly from genuine encounters with Kynari.

The descriptions were approximate rather than anatomically accurate. To a human without any concept of alien biology, describing a Kynari as a person with the head of a dog was considerably easier than describing an entirely unfamiliar species. As stories traveled farther from the original encounters, this simplified description became increasingly literal.

Artists who had never seen a Kynari eventually depicted the Cynocephali as ordinary human bodies with canine heads. Real Kynari never looked like that. Their entire anatomy, posture, proportions, face, limbs, tail, and movement were distinctly their own.

Over time, genuine Kynari encounters also became mixed with unrelated stories about distant peoples, monsters, strange animals, and imagined civilizations. As a result, only some Cynocephali traditions have any actual connection to the Kynari.

## Werewolves
Fragments of old Kynari encounters may also have contributed to later stories involving werewolves and other canine humanoids. The connection is indirect, and there is no single alien explanation for werewolf folklore.

Kynari do not transform between human and animal forms, and most werewolf traditions developed independently. However, older stories describing intelligent canine-like people survived for centuries and were occasionally absorbed into later beliefs involving transformation, curses, spirits, monsters, or dangerous wilderness creatures.

The result is a mixture of unrelated human folklore into which genuine ancient encounters were occasionally folded.

## The Aezorgh
Kynari history with the [[Aezorgh]] is considerably darker. Numerous Kynari governments, military powers, colonial organizations, and independent groups conquered Aezorgh populations during different periods of expansion. These conquests included slavery, forced relocation, seizure of territory, destruction of local governments, and the movement of entire Aezorgh communities between worlds.

There was never a single unified Kynari policy toward the Aezorgh because Kynari civilization has rarely possessed enough political unity for such a policy to exist. Instead, similar abuses occurred repeatedly under different states, organizations, and leaders.

Some modern Aezorgh populations live where they do specifically because their ancestors were forcibly transported there by Kynari. Modern relations between the two species consequently vary enormously. Some communities have lived peacefully beside one another for generations, while others retain territorial disputes, historical grievances, cultural hostility, or political conflicts directly connected to earlier periods of conquest.

## Relations with Humanity
Kynari and humans generally find one another relatively easy to understand socially despite their biological differences. Both species readily form small groups, friendships outside biological families, professional crews, military units, informal alliances, and networks based primarily upon personal trust.

Their social instincts are not identical, however. Humans frequently underestimate how quickly status can change within a Kynari group, while Kynari can struggle with human systems in which formal authority remains intact regardless of personal reputation. A human officer can remain legally in command even when virtually everyone beneath them believes they are incompetent, something many Kynari find difficult to understand.

Humans also regularly miss large portions of Kynari body language. Ear position, tail movement, posture, scent, and changes in vocal tone can communicate information that a human never notices. Kynari face the reverse problem with humans and usually require experience before subtle human facial expressions become intuitive.

## Kynari Travellers
Kynari are extremely common Travellers. Their fragmented civilization produces merchants, scouts, mercenaries, engineers, soldiers, scientists, physicians, criminals, explorers, diplomats, prospectors, pilots, and independent starship crews in enormous numbers.

Travel also provides opportunities for Kynari who possess little influence at home. Someone with few connections can leave, build a reputation elsewhere, gather companions and resources, and return years later carrying far greater social influence than they possessed before departing. Many simply never return and establish new lives elsewhere.

Small starship crews fit Kynari social instincts particularly well. Everyone aboard knows one another, competence is immediately visible, survival requires mutual trust, and long periods spent together naturally produce intense personal relationships. For many Kynari, the distinction between a crew and a pack eventually becomes mostly semantic.

## Characteristics
Kynari Travellers have the following modifiers applied to  
their characteristics: STR-1, DEX+1, END-1

## Traits
Kynari Travellers all possess the following traits.

## Bite: 
All Kynari possess pronounced canines which make for a nasty close combat weapon. This bite uses the  Melee (natural) skill and does 1D+1 damage.

## Heightened Senses: 
Kynari have better hearing and sense of smell than humans. All Kynari receive DM+1 to any Recon  
and Survival checks they have to make. However, their eyesight is worse in darkness and they suffer DM-1 to any skill check requiring sight in dark conditions.
