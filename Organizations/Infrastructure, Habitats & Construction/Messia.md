**Messia** is a specialist biodome engineering company headquartered at [[Solace Station]] in [[Cislunar Space]]. The company designs, constructs, and maintains self-contained living environments for orbital installations, planetary settlements, agricultural operations, research sites, and remote outposts.

Messia's work focuses on the dome itself and the systems required to make life inside it sustainable. Its projects combine pressure structures, climate regulation, water cycling, atmosphere management, soil systems, plant life, food production, and other ecological infrastructure into enclosed environments capable of supporting permanent populations.

The company's orbital biodomes provide large enclosed living spaces for stations and habitats that would otherwise depend entirely on conventional sealed compartments. Surface biodomes perform a similar role on planets and moons with unsuitable atmospheres, hostile climates, or otherwise difficult surface conditions.

Agricultural domes are designed specifically for dependable food production, while residential biomes combine housing with controlled natural environments. Messia also builds dedicated research environments where scientists can study controlled ecosystems, agricultural systems, biological processes, or environmental conditions without exposing the surrounding settlement.

Closed-loop ecology is central to Messia's engineering. Water, air, nutrients, waste, and biological systems are designed to circulate within the dome with as little external dependence as practical. Large installations can therefore function as substantial pieces of settlement infrastructure rather than decorative green spaces.

Messia does **not** terraform planets. Its business model is based on creating controlled habitable environments within otherwise unsuitable locations rather than altering an entire planetary ecosystem. This places it alongside habitat developers such as [[Akers Multistellar]], while giving Messia a much narrower specialization in enclosed biological environments.
