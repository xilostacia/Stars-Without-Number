The **Aezorgh** are a small hexapodal sophont species known throughout inhabited space for their dexterity, adaptability, climbing ability, and habit of turning up almost anywhere there is work, trade, machinery, or something interesting to get into. They are considerably smaller and physically weaker than humans, but compensate with exceptional coordination, four highly dexterous arms, excellent situational awareness, and an ability to move across walls and ceilings with unnerving ease.

Aezorgh are a fully independent extraterrestrial species rather than an uplift or engineered people. Their modern population is widely dispersed, in large part because of a long history of enslavement and displacement by the canid species humanity currently knows under the provisional name [[Kynari]]. Today, Aezorgh can be found far beyond their original homeworld, particularly around ports, trade routes, frontier settlements, engineering communities, and regions historically influenced by their former masters. 

## Biology
Aezorgh are small six-limbed creatures with **two primary walking limbs and four dexterous arms**. Their compact size makes them physically unimposing beside a human, but their anatomy gives them extraordinary control over their surroundings. An Aezorgh can manipulate several objects simultaneously, hold itself in place while working with its remaining limbs, or perform complicated maintenance tasks in spaces that would be extremely awkward for a human technician.

Each manipulatory limb ends in flexible, almost tentacle-like fingers capable of using tools designed for a wide range of species. Their hands are not simply miniature human hands, and an Aezorgh using equipment often appears to wrap or curl its digits around controls rather than grasping them in the human manner. Despite these differences, they are unusually capable of adapting to machinery not specifically designed for them.

Their most visually distinctive adaptation is the network of specialized gripping ridges along their feet and wrists. These structures allow an Aezorgh to adhere to surfaces in a manner often compared to terrestrial geckos. Under ordinary gravity, they can move vertically across walls with little reduction in speed and can cross ceilings almost as naturally as humans walk along a floor. The ability remains effective under gravity somewhat greater than Earth's, although sufficiently heavy conditions eventually overcome it.

This ability has a major effect on Aezorgh behavior. They do not instinctively divide an interior into "floor" and "walls" as rigidly as humans do. Pipes, overhead structures, maintenance spaces, structural supports, and exposed machinery can all become convenient routes through a room. An Aezorgh aboard a human vessel may simply climb over equipment rather than walk around it, and finding one working upside down above a corridor is entirely normal.

## Senses
Aezorgh vision extends significantly into the **infrared spectrum**. They are extremely good at detecting heat sources and navigating poorly illuminated environments, particularly when machinery, living organisms, or other warm objects are present. Darkness that severely limits human vision may be little more than an inconvenience to an Aezorgh.

Their wide field of sensory awareness also makes them naturally observant. They tend to notice movement, environmental changes, unfamiliar sounds, mechanical irregularities, and potential routes through an area quickly. This has contributed to their reputation as excellent scouts, mechanics, thieves, explorers, and troublemakers, depending almost entirely on who is describing them.

Infrared perception does not mean they see perfectly in absolute darkness. A completely thermally uniform environment can still be difficult to interpret, and equipment designed to conceal heat can be effective against them. Their vision is simply built around a broader range of information than ordinary human sight.

## Reproduction and Development
Aezorgh possess two biological sexes, with females producing eggs. Reproduction is relatively infrequent, with fertile cycles separated by roughly a year and a half. Eggs can be carried through development naturally or maintained in artificial incubators, which has made reproduction considerably easier for Aezorgh living aboard ships or in mixed-species settlements.

Raising a young Aezorgh is a substantial commitment. They take nearly two decades to reach full adult independence, and Aezorgh families generally treat child-rearing as a long-term responsibility rather than something undertaken casually. Incubator technology makes the biological portion of reproduction relatively manageable, but it does nothing to shorten childhood.

Aezorgh remain physically capable for a surprisingly long time relative to their size. They typically reach adulthood at about the same age humans do but often show relatively little physical decline until well into later life. Individuals remaining active into their eighties are entirely unremarkable.

## Bands
Traditional Aezorgh society revolves around small social groups commonly translated as **bands**. A band may contain biological relatives, long-term companions, adopted members, business partners, or some combination of all four. The exact structure varies enormously between communities, particularly among populations that have lived away from the Aezorgh homeworld for generations.

A band provides social belonging, mutual assistance, education, protection, and practical support, but it is not intended to contain an individual for life. Young Aezorgh have a strong cultural tendency to leave their home band for a time, seeking employment, adventure, education, wealth, experience, or simply something they have not seen before. Traveller accounts frequently note this tendency as one reason Aezorgh are encountered so widely despite their comparatively small population.

Some eventually return to their original band. Others establish new ones or settle permanently among other species. Aezorgh therefore have a long tradition of migration and of maintaining relationships across considerable distances.

## History of Enslavement
Aezorgh history cannot be separated from their long relationship with the canid sophonts presently represented by the Traveller term [[Kynari]].

Aezorgh populations were conquered, enslaved, transported, bought, sold, and dispersed across numerous settlements under canid control. Over time, this resulted in large populations living far from their original homeworld and eventually left the Aezorgh themselves as a minority in parts of their ancestral territory. Even after widespread slavery ended, its demographic consequences remained.

Their very name originated during this period. **Aezorgh** derives from an old canid term roughly meaning **"vermin."** Rather than discard the insult, later generations adopted it as their own name and turned it into a deliberate badge of identity. The continued use of the word is partly cultural defiance: their former masters intended it to diminish them, so the Aezorgh kept it.

This history still shapes relations between the two species. Individual Aezorgh and canids can obviously work together, trade, become friends, or live in the same communities, but the historical relationship has not vanished simply because formal enslavement did.

## Reputation
Aezorgh have acquired a widespread reputation for being **clever, opportunistic, difficult to contain, and occasionally devious**. Some of this reputation is deserved. They tend to value adaptability and are very good at finding unconventional solutions to practical problems.

A considerable portion of it, however, is inherited prejudice.

Their former enslavers frequently characterized them as vermin, thieves, scavengers, and untrustworthy servants. Those stereotypes spread alongside displaced Aezorgh populations and survived long after the institutions that created them. Even today, an Aezorgh crawling through a maintenance duct may be regarded with suspicion despite the fact that a human engineer doing exactly the same thing would simply be considered to be doing their job.

The result is that Aezorgh are often **tolerated before they are trusted**, particularly in places with little direct experience of them.

Aezorgh themselves are well aware of this reputation and react to it in different ways. Some make a point of being scrupulously professional. Others deliberately play into the stereotype because people who already expect you to be sneaky are surprisingly easy to distract from what you are actually doing.

## Technology and Work
Aezorgh are not associated with a single dominant technological specialty, but their physiology naturally lends itself to certain kinds of work. Four manipulatory arms allow them to perform multiple related tasks at once, while their climbing ability lets them reach machinery without scaffolding or specialized access equipment.

They are therefore commonly found working as mechanics, engineers, maintenance specialists, technicians, scouts, pilots, salvage workers, explorers, and operators of small machinery. Their compact bodies are particularly useful aboard spacecraft, where important systems are frequently packed into spaces designed more for machinery than for the people expected to repair it.

Aezorgh technicians have a reputation for treating maintenance access routes as suggestions. A human engineer may open a service panel. An Aezorgh may disappear behind the reactor shielding and emerge twenty minutes later from somewhere nobody realized was connected to it. This does not necessarily mean the Aezorgh ignored safety procedures. It frequently means the safety procedures did not anticipate an engineer capable of walking across the ceiling.

## Ships and Habitats
Aezorgh can use human-scale environments with relatively little modification. They breathe compatible atmospheres, operate comfortably under common planetary gravities, and can handle equipment designed for human-sized manipulators surprisingly well.

Purpose-built Aezorgh spaces look considerably different.

Vertical surfaces become useful living space. Storage may be mounted high above the apparent floor, workstations can be arranged at several orientations, and passages that humans would consider ladders or maintenance shafts may function as ordinary corridors. A room may contain equipment across every available surface because an Aezorgh does not need everything placed between waist and shoulder height.

Seats are also less important than they are to humans. An Aezorgh can simply anchor itself securely to a wall, ceiling, frame, or padded support when necessary. Mixed-species ships tend to compromise. Aezorgh crew quarters may contain overhead storage, climbing surfaces, and elevated work areas while the rest of the vessel remains conventionally oriented for humans.

## Relations with Humans
Aezorgh generally adapt well to human society. Their environmental requirements are modest, their tools can usually be adapted easily, and their long history among other species has produced cultures accustomed to navigating foreign laws and customs.

Humans tend to find them simultaneously endearing and unsettling. Their small size and energetic behavior can make them seem harmless, right up until one crosses the ceiling above someone's head without warning or reaches around a machine with four arms while discussing three unrelated problems.

Aezorgh also have little difficulty understanding that humans cannot climb the way they can, although remembering this in the moment is another matter. Instructions such as "just go through the upper maintenance trunk" are occasionally followed by an awkward silence before the Aezorgh remembers who it is talking to.

## Individuals
There is no single Aezorgh personality. Their reputation for cleverness and opportunism describes broad tendencies and historical stereotypes rather than a biological compulsion.

An Aezorgh might be a merchant, engineer, scientist, thief, doctor, scout, courier, criminal, pilot, academic, miner, investigator, entertainer, or simply someone who left home because staying there sounded boring.

Young adults travelling away from their bands are particularly common among those encountered aboard ships. For many Aezorgh, spending several years wandering through unfamiliar systems is not evidence that something went wrong with their life. It may simply be what they were expected to do with it.

## Full Rules
JTAS Volume 11 (Do Not Own)

Characteristics
Initial Aezorgh characteristics are rolled as: STR 1D, DEX 2D+3, END 1D+1, INT 2D+2, EDU 1D, SOC 1D. The maximum for DEX is 18 and INT is 16.

Instead of standard background skill determination, all Aezorgh gain Athletics 0, Mechanic 0, Recon 0, Stealth 0 and one additional skill of their choice.

Traits
Aezorgh Travellers all possess the following traits:

Aging (+4)

Gecko Travel: Ridges on an Aezorgh’s feet and wrists allow the Traveller to climb walls at full movement speed and to cross ceilings at half movement speed in gravity up to 1.5 standard.

Heightened Senses (IR 1): Wide infrared vision and innate situational awareness allow Aezorgh to receive DM+1 to Recon or Survival checks. If a heat source is present, the Aezorgh’s infrared vision negates any penalty for darkness.

Multi-limbed (2): Aezorgh have four dexterous arms, allowing them to perform two sets of non-movement actions in any round without penalty. Aezorgh tentacle fingers are flexible enough to allow the use of tools and equipment designed for other species without penalty.
