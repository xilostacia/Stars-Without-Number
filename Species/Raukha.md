The **Raukha** are a widespread starfaring species whose appearance is often described by humans as feline, although the comparison is an approximation rather than a literal biological relationship. They are not descended from terrestrial felines and are not simply humanoid lions or cats. Their evolutionary history is entirely separate from that of [[Earth]], but produced a number of superficially similar traits, particularly in the structure of the face, ears, eyes, teeth, claws, coat, and general predatory build.

Raukha civilization predates humanity's expansion into interstellar space and was already capable of faster-than-light travel while Earth was still home to early agricultural civilizations. Their early period of expansion was considerably more aggressive than their modern reputation might suggest, with individual clans, military expeditions, and colonial ventures spreading far beyond their home territory. One such expedition reached Earth several thousand years before the modern era, conquered ancient Egypt, and was ultimately destroyed by the humans it had attempted to rule.

Modern Raukha society remains strongly organized around family, clan, ancestry, territory, personal reputation, and the obligations created between individuals. They possess no single political structure equivalent to a centralized human nation-state governing the entire species. Raukha civilization instead consists of countless overlapping clans, territories, alliances, settlements, client communities, commercial interests, and political relationships whose boundaries frequently matter more to a Raukha than any line drawn on a stellar map.

## Biology
Raukha are upright, warm-blooded sophonts with a broadly humanoid body plan, but they are visibly nonhuman from head to foot. Their musculature, skeletal proportions, posture, hands, feet, spinal structure, and movement developed independently of humanity. A Raukha should not be imagined as a human body with the head and tail of an Earth animal attached to it.

The face is usually the feature humans notice first. Raukha possess forward-facing eyes, prominent mobile ears, powerful jaw musculature, pronounced canine teeth, and a short to moderate muzzle. Their noses, facial structure, whisker-like sensory hairs, and the arrangement of the muscles surrounding the mouth and eyes often remind humans of terrestrial predatory mammals. Their expressions can therefore appear strangely familiar while still being difficult for humans to interpret correctly.

The Raukha body is covered in fur, although its thickness and length vary considerably between populations. Most have a visible tail used heavily for balance and nonverbal communication. Their legs are built differently from those of humans and produce a distinctive gait, particularly when running, while the spine possesses greater flexibility through the torso than is typical of humanity.

Raukha hands remain capable of fine manipulation and the use of complex machinery, but their digits are heavier and less naturally suited to some forms of delicate manual work than human hands. Each hand also possesses an extendable fighting claw located separately from the ordinary claws of the fingers. This structure is commonly called the **dewclaw** when translated into human languages and is capable of inflicting serious wounds at extremely close range.

### Physical Variation
Raukha display considerable natural variation across their species. Coat color, patterning, facial proportions, ear shape, body size, limb proportions, fur length, neck and facial hair, tail structure, and other visible traits can differ substantially between populations and individuals.

Human observers frequently compare different Raukha to different terrestrial felines. One individual may be described as lion-like because of a broad face, heavy build, and thick growth around the head and neck, while another may be compared to a leopard because of a narrower frame and patterned coat. Others may have stripes, rosettes, spots, solid coats, pale facial markings, dark facial markings, pronounced ruffs, nearly uniform short fur, or combinations for which no close Earth comparison exists.

These similarities do not represent separate subspecies corresponding to Earth's lions, leopards, panthers, or other felines. Those animals have no evolutionary relationship to the Raukha. Human comparisons are simply convenient descriptions of a much broader range of Raukha phenotypes.

The degree of variation is roughly comparable to the obvious physical variation found among human populations, although Raukha differences are often more visually dramatic because pigmentation is expressed across the entire coat rather than primarily through exposed skin, hair, and facial features. No particular appearance determines clan, social position, occupation, or temperament.

## Senses
Raukha senses developed around a predatory evolutionary history. Their eyesight functions well under low-light conditions, and their hearing covers a broader useful range than that of humans. Smell also remains an important environmental and social sense rather than the greatly reduced sense possessed by most humans.

This combination makes Raukha particularly capable scouts and wilderness travelers. They tend to notice movement, unfamiliar smells, disturbed environments, and nearby living creatures quickly, although none of these abilities approach supernatural levels. Technological societies have reduced the survival importance of these traits, but they remain useful enough to influence Raukha habits and the design of Raukha equipment.

Raukha ships and settlements often account for sensory cues that humans overlook. Ventilation, personal scent, acoustic privacy, and lighting can all carry social implications that are not immediately apparent to outsiders.

## Diet
Raukha evolved as predators and remain heavily dependent upon animal protein. Modern food technology allows considerable flexibility in exactly how this requirement is met, and starfaring Raukha do not require freshly killed prey or anything similarly impractical. Cultured meat, preserved protein, synthesized food products, and imported livestock products are commonplace.

Food remains culturally significant in many Raukha communities, particularly meals associated with hospitality, family gatherings, negotiations, and celebrations. Sharing food can carry implications of trust or temporary peace, although the precise customs vary enormously between clans.

Humans sometimes interpret Raukha eating habits as unusually aggressive or ritualized because of their dentition and predatory anatomy. Most Raukha find this reaction amusing at best and insulting at worst.

## Family
The family is the smallest major unit of Raukha social organization, but a Raukha family is rarely viewed as an isolated household. Every immediate family exists within a much larger network of relatives, ancestors, marriages, obligations, patrons, dependents, and clan relationships.

Children grow up with a strong awareness of where they belong within that network. Genealogy is not merely a matter of curiosity. It can determine inheritance, political allegiance, responsibility for debts, rights to property, expectations of assistance, and membership within a clan.

Adoption is possible and socially meaningful. A person accepted fully into a family can acquire genuine obligations and privileges through that relationship rather than being permanently treated as an outsider. Raukha therefore distinguish between simple biological ancestry and socially recognized kinship, although individual clans differ greatly in how easily the latter is granted.

## Clans
The **clan** is the central institution of Raukha civilization. Clans range from relatively small extended families controlling a settlement or commercial enterprise to enormous organizations possessing populations spread across multiple worlds. A large clan can simultaneously resemble a noble house, corporation, government, military organization, family network, and political party without being identical to any of them.

A Raukha normally identifies strongly with their clan. The clan provides protection, employment, political representation, access to shared resources, legal support, and a network of individuals expected to assist one another. In return, members carry obligations to the clan and can be held responsible for conduct that damages its interests or reputation.

Clans are not internally uniform. Branch families frequently compete for influence, and disagreements that would constitute political disputes in a human state may occur entirely within a single clan. Large clans can contain internal alliances, rivalries, regional blocs, commercial interests, military factions, and ideological disagreements extending across generations.

Relations between clans range from close alliance to open warfare. Marriage, trade agreements, territorial exchanges, shared ventures, arbitration, compensation, hostage exchanges, duels, and negotiated settlements all form part of the political system surrounding these relationships.

## Territory
Ownership and control of territory have enormous cultural importance to the Raukha. Territory is not limited to farmland or planetary surface area. It may include cities, orbital facilities, resource rights, asteroid claims, stations, habitats, shipping concessions, industrial zones, entire worlds, or recognized authority over particular regions of space.

Territory provides both practical resources and social legitimacy. A powerful clan is expected to possess something tangible over which it exercises recognized authority. Losing territory can therefore represent more than an economic defeat, while gaining new territory can elevate the position of an entire family line.

This historical emphasis on territory was one of the major forces behind early Raukha interstellar expansion. Young or politically marginal branches of established clans often sought opportunities beyond existing Raukha space because acquiring new territory offered a path to independence and status that was difficult to achieve at home.

This tradition produced generations of explorers, colonists, mercenaries, conquerors, and adventurers. It also created some of the most destructive periods in Raukha history, when competing expeditions treated inhabited worlds as potential possessions rather than independent civilizations.

## Status and Reputation
Raukha society places considerable importance on reputation. What an individual has done, whom they represent, which obligations they have fulfilled, and whether their word can be trusted can matter as much as formal wealth or rank.

Insults are therefore not always treated as trivial social unpleasantness. A public accusation of cowardice, dishonesty, disloyalty, or failure may damage a Raukha's position within relationships that have material consequences. The appropriate response depends heavily upon circumstance, status, audience, and clan tradition.

This does not mean every Raukha responds violently to disrespect. Formal apologies, restitution, mediation, compensation, public correction, and negotiated settlements are all normal ways of resolving disputes. Violence becomes more likely when the insult challenges something the individual believes cannot be surrendered without permanently accepting a lower position.

Outsiders sometimes characterize Raukha culture simply as being based upon "honor." Raukha generally consider this an oversimplification. Reputation matters because Raukha society is built upon relationships and obligations, and a person's reputation determines whether others believe those obligations will be fulfilled.

## Violence and Duels
Raukha possess a strong martial tradition, partly inherited from their predatory ancestry and partly produced by centuries of competition between clans. Their natural weapons mean even an apparently unarmed Raukha is capable of inflicting lethal injuries at close range.

Formalized violence developed in part to keep personal disputes from becoming clan disputes. Depending upon local custom, a challenge may be settled through a duel, a contest, payment, arbitration, or another agreed method. The purpose is not necessarily to kill the opponent but to produce a recognized outcome that allows everyone involved to consider the dispute resolved.

Actual clan warfare is far more serious. Modern Raukha generally understand the enormous economic and political cost of unrestricted conflict, particularly once interstellar weapons are involved. Most rivalries therefore remain limited, indirect, or subject to negotiated restrictions unless relations have deteriorated completely.

## Government
There is no universal Raukha government.

Raukha territory is divided among clans and the political organizations those clans create. A single world might be controlled by one dominant clan, partitioned between several clans, governed through a council of local families, or operated through a complicated network of property rights and contractual authority.

Larger political structures are usually alliances rather than true centralized states. Powerful clans may recognize common procedures for diplomacy, trade, warfare, travel, arbitration, and dealing with foreign civilizations while fiercely resisting any institution capable of overruling them within their own territory.

To outsiders, this can make Raukha politics appear disorganized. In practice, the system is highly structured but based upon relationships rather than a single hierarchy. Knowing who formally owns something is often less useful than knowing which clan controls it, which other clans recognize that control, what agreements protect it, and who is obligated to intervene if it is threatened.

## Law
Raukha law varies considerably between territories. Clan law normally governs members of the clan, while agreements between clans establish procedures for dealing with disputes involving multiple groups. Foreigners may fall under local territorial law, treaty provisions, or the authority of whoever has formally accepted responsibility for them.

Responsibility is an important legal concept. A Raukha who brings an outsider into a restricted settlement may become responsible for that person's conduct. A captain may be responsible for the crew of their ship, an employer for certain actions of an employee, or a family for a member whose behavior creates a wider dispute.

This system can be confusing to humans accustomed to impersonal legal institutions. To the Raukha, however, knowing who stands behind an individual is often more important than knowing which bureaucratic office technically has jurisdiction.

## Technology
Modern Raukha technology is fully interstellar and broadly compatible with the technological standards used by other major starfaring civilizations. Their ships, computers, weapons, vehicles, medical equipment, and industrial systems are not based upon mysterious or fundamentally incompatible principles.

Raukha design does, however, reflect Raukha anatomy and social expectations. Controls intended for Raukha hands can be uncomfortable for humans, seating accounts for tails and different leg structure, and living spaces often incorporate different assumptions about smell, sound, privacy, and personal distance.

Ships belonging to major clans commonly carry visible identifiers showing ownership and affiliation. Even vessels technically belonging to individuals may prominently display clan associations because the distinction between personal property and family property can be politically important.

## Early Interstellar Expansion
The Raukha achieved faster-than-light travel comparatively early in their technological development. Their first interstellar ships were crude by modern standards, but even primitive FTL capability gave them access to worlds whose inhabitants possessed no comparable technology.

This period encouraged aggressive expansion. Individual clans and military ventures could travel beyond established authority, claim territory, and return vastly wealthier than they had left. Worlds without starfaring civilizations were especially attractive targets because early Raukha expansion did not universally recognize technologically primitive inhabitants as possessing political rights equal to those of Raukha clans.

Some worlds were settled peacefully. Others were coerced, exploited, or conquered.

The consequences of this era remain visible throughout Raukha history. Modern clans often possess claims whose origins lie in early colonial expeditions, while some neighboring species remember the Raukha primarily through conflicts with those same expeditions.

## Earth
One of the strangest events of the early expansion period involved a small blue world that would later be known to its inhabitants as [[Earth]].

A Raukha military expedition discovered humanity while humans were still divided among early civilizations with no industrial technology, no advanced weapons, and no knowledge of life beyond their own world. The expedition judged Earth to be an exceptionally easy target. The disparity in technology was so extreme that the possibility of complete military failure appears to have received little serious consideration.

The Raukha invaded.

Their primary campaign was directed against ancient Egypt. Human forces could not meet Raukha weapons and vehicles on equal terms, and the initial war ended exactly as the invaders expected. Egypt was defeated and occupied.

The expedition had won.

Holding what it had conquered proved considerably more difficult.

## Bastet's Expedition
The invasion was led by a Raukha commander remembered by surviving records as **Bastet**. Two other prominent members of the expedition were **Mafdet** and **Mau**, although surviving Raukha accounts provide only an incomplete picture of their exact positions and responsibilities.

The occupation initially appears to have been considered successful. The Raukha controlled the major centers they required, possessed overwhelming technological superiority, and faced a population with no realistic means of duplicating most of their equipment.

Human resistance nevertheless continued.

The humans could not defeat the Raukha by fighting the kind of war the Raukha expected them to fight. Instead, resistance persisted through ambushes, sabotage, theft, attacks against isolated personnel, exploitation of local geography, disruption of supplies, assassination, and the gradual identification of weaknesses in Raukha equipment and procedures.

The occupation force could kill humans far more easily than humans could kill Raukha, but there were enormously more humans than Raukha. Every Raukha casualty represented a trained member of an expedition operating an immense distance from home. Human losses could be horrific without ending the resistance.

At some point the occupation ceased being a conquest and became a prolonged fight for survival.

The humans eventually destroyed the expedition.

Bastet, Mafdet, Mau, and the other Raukha on Earth were killed. No permanent colony survived, and no functioning Raukha authority remained on the planet.

## The Blacklisting of Earth
The destruction of the expedition had a profound effect upon later Raukha policy.

The military significance of the loss was not that humanity possessed superior weapons. They plainly did not. What disturbed the Raukha was that a technologically primitive population had absorbed the initial defeat, endured occupation by a starfaring species, adapted to circumstances it could barely understand, and eventually eliminated the force that had conquered it.

A second expedition could almost certainly have retaken the planet. The Raukha chose not to send one.

Earth was instead designated as a world to be avoided. The exact terminology changed over the centuries, but the practical rule remained remarkably consistent: **do not attempt to conquer Earth, do not establish unnecessary settlements there, and do not involve a clan in whatever happened to Bastet's expedition.**

The decision eventually outlived the political circumstances that created it. Long after the original invasion became ancient history, Raukha navigators, clan records, and historical traditions continued to identify Earth as a place where intervention was considered unusually unwise.

Among many Raukha, the event became less a military case study and more a cultural warning about arrogance.

## Egyptian Legacy
Humanity did not preserve an accurate historical account of the invasion.

Memories of the Raukha survived in Egyptian traditions long after the occupation itself had disappeared. Bastet, Mafdet, and Mau became detached from their original identities and gradually entered mythology as supernatural or divine figures.

The feline appearance of the Raukha contributed heavily to the way they were remembered. Human artists interpreted alien anatomy through familiar terrestrial forms, transforming Raukha features into stylized animal heads attached to idealized human bodies. These images should not be regarded as accurate depictions of what the Raukha actually looked like.

The existence of the Raukha also provided ancient Egyptians with a real precedent for intelligent beings whose appearance combined traits humans associated with people and animals. Later animal-headed Egyptian deities did not therefore need to represent additional extraterrestrial species. Falcon-headed, crocodile-headed, jackal-headed, ibis-headed, and other divine forms remained products of human religion, but developed within a culture whose distant ancestors had genuinely encountered nonhuman people.

By the historical period, the distinction between occupier, ruler, monster, ancestor, and god had long since disappeared.

## Humanity
Modern relations between humans and Raukha are complicated by the enormous gap between the two species' memories of their first encounter.

For humanity, the Raukha invasion disappeared into prehistory. Bastet is remembered as an Egyptian deity rather than the commander of an extraterrestrial occupation force, and the idea that Egypt was briefly conquered by aliens is not part of conventional human history.

The Raukha never forgot completely.

Knowledge of the invasion varies between clans, scholars, and regions, and many modern Raukha regard it as little more than an unusual episode from their species' early expansion. Others retain a much stronger cultural awareness of Earth as the world where Bastet's expedition was destroyed.

Humanity's later appearance as an independent interstellar civilization therefore creates an uncomfortable historical reversal. The species once considered too technologically primitive to matter eventually reached the stars without Raukha assistance and encountered the descendants of the people who had once attempted to conquer it.

## Relations with Other Species
Raukha attitudes toward other sophonts vary heavily by clan and historical experience. Their civilization is too decentralized for a single foreign policy to exist across the entire species.

Some clans have maintained peaceful commercial relationships with neighboring species for centuries, while others have histories of territorial conflict, raiding, colonial expansion, or military rivalry. A species considered a trusted ally by one clan may be treated as an enemy by another.

Raukha generally understand collective species labels but tend to think politically in terms of actual organizations. Saying that the Raukha are allied with or hostile toward another species is therefore often less useful than identifying which Raukha clans and which foreign governments are actually involved.

## Raukha Travellers
Raukha leave their home territories for many of the same reasons humans do. Some travel as merchants, scouts, soldiers, scientists, diplomats, engineers, mercenaries, explorers, criminals, or simply individuals who do not fit comfortably into the expectations of their families.

Younger Raukha without substantial inheritance or territory have historically been especially likely to travel. Interstellar space provides opportunities to acquire wealth, reputation, property, connections, and independence that may be unavailable within an established clan hierarchy.

A Raukha Traveller normally retains some connection to their family and clan even while operating independently. Whether that connection is supportive, burdensome, hostile, politically useful, or deliberately avoided depends upon the individual.

## Characteristics

Raukha Travellers have the following modifiers applied to their characteristics:
**STR +2, DEX -2**

## Traits
Raukha Travellers all possess the following traits.

### Dewclaw
All Raukha have a dewclaw which can be extended to make for a vicious close combat weapon. The dewclaw uses the **Melee (natural)** skill and does **1D+2 damage**.

### Heightened Senses
Raukha have better night vision, hearing and sense of smell than humans. All Raukha receive **DM+1 to any Recon and Survival checks** they have to make.
